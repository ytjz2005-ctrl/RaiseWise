GOOD QUESTIONS

A few things
worth knowing.

How do I calculate my pay raise percentage?+

Subtract your old pay from your new pay, divide by your old pay, then multiply by 100. Moving from $60,000 to $63,000 is a 5% raise: ($63,000 − $60,000) ÷ $60,000 × 100. Choose “New pay” above to do this automatically.

Is this my actual take-home pay?+

No. All amounts are gross pay, before income taxes, insurance, retirement contributions, or other deductions. Your actual increase in take-home pay will depend on your circumstances. This calculator does not apply tax rules.

Why are “every two weeks” and “twice a month” different?+

Every two weeks generally means 26 paychecks per year; twice a month means 24. A $3,000 annual raise is about $115.38 per biweekly paycheck or $125 per semimonthly paycheck. We use a standard 26-payment year; an occasional 27-paycheck year is not modeled.

Can I compare hourly wages or a pay cut?+

Yes. Choose “Hourly wage” and set your paid hours and weeks under “Work schedule & inflation.” You can enter a negative raise or a lower new pay to model a pay cut. Hourly estimates use a constant schedule and do not add overtime premiums.

Where are my saved scenarios stored?+

In this browser’s local storage. We do not upload your salary or scenarios. They do not sync across devices, and clearing browser data removes them. Export a CSV if you want a copy to keep. You can turn off remembered inputs and clear saved scenarios independently.
